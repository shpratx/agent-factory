Platform Engineering — the product/platform build agents that carry an idea from vision through requirements, PI planning, inception, design, test strategy, and construction. These are the content-generating agents of the greenfield SDLC.
Agents (35)
Phase 0 — Vision
L1-vision-idea-intake
Takes a raw idea — free-text brief, or a reference to an existing Confluence page — and structures it into a clear problem statement, target user definition, and a set of candidate success metrics. Every downstream agent depends on this producing the same structured starting facts, so each reasons from a shared foundation rather than re-interpreting the raw idea text and drifting apart.

Inputs

Raw idea brief (free text); sponsoring stakeholder list (optional)
Human: Product Lead submission via intake form or Confluence "Idea Brief" page
External: stakeholder registry (org directory / HRIS export)

Outputs

idea_brief items (JSON only)
Tools: tool-L1-confluence-fetch-pageKnowledge Bases: — (structure embedded in prompt per S4)Guardrails: gr-L1-hallucination-check, gr-L1-consistency-check
L1-vision-market-analyzer
Given the problem statement and target users, performs a five-dimension market analysis: competitive intelligence (competitor matrix), market sizing (TAM/SAM/SOM), industry trends, customer insights, and pricing benchmarks — plus a SWOT synthesis. Every citable figure is held to the citation-verifier as a BLOCKER: an ungrounded number is reported as a low-confidence gap, never fabricated.

Inputs

Problem statement + target market; known competitor list (optional)
Agent: idea_brief items from idea-intake
External: live web search; KB: prior competitor/market/pricing knowledge

Outputs

market_analysis items (competitor_matrix, market_sizing, industry_trends, customer_insights, pricing_benchmarks, swot, data_sufficiency)
Tools: tool-L1-web-search-competitor-scanKnowledge Bases: kb-L2-domain-marketGuardrails: gr-L1-hallucination-check, gr-L1-citation-verifier
L1-vision-regulatory-feasibility-checker
Assesses regulatory feasibility against the licensing and compliance regimes relevant to the target geography and product category, classifying each constraint Green/Amber/Red with a citation and mitigation. Runs under a zero-tolerance rule for omitting a Red-classified constraint — a false negative here is a compliance risk, not a quality slip.

Inputs

Problem statement + target geography/product category
Agent: idea_brief items from idea-intake
External: regulatory database/API lookup

Outputs

regulatory_feasibility items (reconciled into vision.md § Regulatory Posture)
Tools: tool-L1-regulatory-db-lookupKnowledge Bases: kb-L1-regulatory-frameworks-index, kb-L2-domain-regulatoryGuardrails: gr-L1-hallucination-check, gr-L1-citation-verifier
L1-vision-statement-generator
Synthesizes idea brief, market analysis, and regulatory feasibility — plus the viability score from the QG — into one reconciled vision statement and roadmap outline carrying a north-star metric and a Regulatory Posture section. It is the last automated checkpoint that could catch a dropped regulatory finding before a human reviewer.

Inputs

Idea brief; market analysis; regulatory feasibility; viability score
Agent: items of idea-intake, market-analyzer, regulatory-feasibility-checker; viability score from L1-quality-verifier

Outputs

vision.md

Converts an approved vision into atomic, traceable functional requirements, refusing to run unless a recorded Product Lead approval comment accompanies the vision. Every downstream step depends on this same atomic requirement set; without it, each agent would re-interpret vision.md independently and drift.

Allocates prioritized features into sprint-bands across the whole PI horizon (e.g. 4 × 5-day Feature Cycles), sequenced by the dependency critical path — the "what and roughly when" a sponsor or cross-cell lead reads. Invariant: a feature may only be banded at or after every feature it depends on, and every backlog feature appears exactly once.

Governance & Quality
L1-quality-verifier
Provides independent, rubric-based scoring at every one of the 18 quality gates across the lifecycle, taking the draft output of whichever agent just ran together with that gate's rubric, and returning a quality score, a pass/fail verdict, and a rationale. It never re-fetches its own inputs, and it is the mechanism by which a blocking gate can actually stop a workflow.

Inputs

Draft output of the agent being scored, plus the rubric for that gate
Agent: the draft artifact from whichever agent just ran (workflow context, not re-fetched)

Outputs

Quality score (0–10) + pass/fail + rationale
